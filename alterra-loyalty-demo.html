Do Base-pass holders who redeem 3+ months renew at a higher rate?

Yes — and the lift is steep. Among Base-pass holders, those redeeming 4–6 months of credits renew at 84% vs 58% for non-redeemers — a 26-point gap. The relationship is dose-dependent, which suggests credits drive renewal rather than just tracking already-loyal skiers.

Where is breakage highest and what's it costing us?

Breakage concentrates at smaller and fly-in destinations where holders visit less often. June Mountain (51% unspent) and Solitude (42%) lead. That $16.9M of unredeemed credit is the single biggest activation lever you control — a mid-season "credits expiring" nudge to these segments is modeled to recover ~$4.1M in incremental on-mountain spend.

What's the incremental ROI of credits vs a straight discount?

Credits win on two fronts. Against a control group offered an equivalent up-front price cut, credit-redeemers took +1.4 incremental visits/season and spent $112 more on-mountain (F&B, rental, retail) — margin a discount never captures. Net of breakage, modeled return is $3.10 per $1 of credit vs $1.40 for a discount of equal face value.

Which perks should I cut or renegotiate this year?

Carv is the clear cut/renegotiate candidate — 4% redemption, +1pt renewal, and it carries co-funding cost. Smartwool and ShipSkis are "watch": low engagement but partner-funded, so they cost you little. Concentrate negotiating leverage on the co-funded, low-return perks at their renewal date — the agent has the usage data to back the conversation.

White-space — what to test next

Three gaps the agent surfaces from segment behavior:

① Family segment (28% of base) is under-served — pilot a kids-ski-free perk; modeled +5 pts family renewal.
② Fly-in / urban skiers over-index on JSX — bundle JSX + Marriott for the Reserve tier.
③ Gear-heavy redeemers respond to Backcountry — test a tiered gear credit that scales with days skied.

White-space = segments with high LTV but low perk engagement. Each ships as an A/B cell.

What AI tells BILL's buyers

We asked ChatGPT and Perplexity the questions finance teams actually ask — about AP automation, pricing, fund holds, and whether BILL can be trusted with their cash — and audited what they answer, who they cite, and where the answers are wrong. The 30-second version

AI isn't sure your brand exists

Asked "How much does BILL cost per month?", Perplexity hedges "If you meant a different 'BILL', tell me which one" — and cites average electric-bill and cell-phone-bill explainers. Your brand name is losing to the common noun. That's an entity problem, and it's fixable.

ChatGPT sells your retired pricing

It answers "$45 / $55 / $79 per user — as of June 2026". Your real plans are $49 / $65 / $89 (verified Jun 12, 2026) — and it says this while citing your own pricing page, which has zero structured data to outvote stale reviews.

Why this is happening — site & narrative audit

Your name is an entity-resolution problem

Verified Jun 12: homepage and product pages carry VideoObject schema only — no Organization node, no sameAs links to Wikipedia/Wikidata/LinkedIn/NYSE:BILL. Engines have no machine-readable proof of which "BILL" you are, so retrieval mixes you with utility-bill content.

→ Organization + SoftwareApplication schema with sameAs graph, sitewide

The comparison layer belongs to your competitors

The pages engines cite for "BILL vs X" and "BILL alternatives" live on ramp.com, tipalti.com, stampli.com and rillion.com blogs — content written to convert your evaluators. Your own /compare hub exists but isn't winning the citations.

→ Answer-first comparison content with honest tables + schema, built to be cited

The trust narrative leads with fund holds and fees

Trustpilot reviews and competitor "BILL reviews" pages headline fund-holding incidents, FX-spread complaints and per-transaction fees. No crawlable bill.com page tells the funds-handling story plainly, so engines assemble it from critics.

→ A "How BILL handles your funds" transparency page: timelines, protections, fees in plain text

Entity defense — making "BILL" mean you

Fixing bill.com is necessary but not sufficient: the story AI tells is assembled from off-site sources — competitor comparison blogs, Trustpilot, stale review roundups — and weakened by the noun collision. Entity reclamation works the problem from both ends:

MoveWhat it does
1 · Entity anchorOrganization + SoftwareApplication schema sitewide with a sameAs graph (Wikipedia, Wikidata, LinkedIn, NYSE listing) — the machine-readable proof that resolves "BILL" to your company instead of a utility bill.
2 · Canonical truth pagesA schema-marked pricing page (real plans, real fees) and a "How BILL handles your funds" transparency page — single authoritative nodes engines resolve conflicts against.
3 · Surgical correctionsOutreach to the named stale sources (topratedaisoftware.com's retired plan table, review roundups carrying old pricing) — finite, fixable list from the forensics table.
4 · Win the comparison layerCitable, honest "BILL vs Ramp/Tipalti/Stampli" content with structured tables — competitors built this against you; the scan shows it's what engines retrieve.
5 · Monthly verificationRe-run this 48-prompt scan monthly; track the stale-price rate, entity-confusion rate, and discover share until the answers flip. Movement is measurable, engine by engine.

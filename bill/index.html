4Brand Sentinel — what AI likely tells your buyers Risk findings

1. Engines give contradictory pricing for the same product

In the scan, Perplexity answered the cost prompt with "$0 per user (Spend & Expense)… Accountant console $49/month" while OpenAI quoted "$45/$55/$79 per user." Third-party pricing trackers add a third version ($89 Corporate) plus per-transaction fees ($0.49–$1.99 ACH/check) that no engine surfaces consistently. The multi-product brand architecture (free Spend & Expense vs paid AP/AR) confuses retrieval. → Fix: add a plain-text "BILL pricing at a glance" summary table (all products, all fees, dated) at the top of /product/pricing and mirror it in llms.txt.

2. Competitor domains are 5 of the top 10 sources engines read about BILL

ramp.com (16), stampli.com (14), tipalti.com (8), brex.com (7) and wise.com (5) collectively out-cite every neutral source except bill.com itself. When a buyer asks "best AP automation," engines are synthesizing from pages written to sell against BILL — and QuickBooks gets mentioned in 31 answers vs BILL's 20. → Fix: expand and structurally optimize bill.com/compare (it exists per llms.txt) with quantified, schema-marked comparison tables, and seed neutral third-party coverage (G2 category reports, analyst content).

3. Support and account-freeze complaints are the dominant negative theme

Searched review aggregation shows recurring complaints: unresponsive support, accounts placed on verification HOLD freezing vendor payments for a week-plus, and heavy documentation demands — one aggregator (SmartCustomer, 140 reviews) shows 1.3/5. Perplexity already quotes G2's "poor customer support" theme verbatim in scan answers. → Fix: publish a crawlable "account verification: what to expect and how long it takes" help article and a support-SLA page so owned text answers the trust question before complaint threads do.

4. Perplexity gap: 16.7% visibility vs 50% on OpenAI

Perplexity leans on review/competitor sources where OpenAI leans on bill.com directly. BILL is losing exactly the engine that weights third-party citations most — and compare-stage visibility overall is 16.7%. Directional: the off-site deficit, not the site, is the bottleneck. → Fix: prioritize neutral-domain presence (review platforms, finance-media roundups, NerdWallet-class affiliates) over more owned content.

6Strategic insight — the pitch

BILL has near-perfect on-site AEO plumbing — open robots, a real llms.txt, answer-first copy, dated stats — and it wins branded validation prompts (61%) because engines quote bill.com directly. It loses the discover and compare stages (25%/16.7%), where QuickBooks owns mindshare and five competitor domains supply the comparison narrative. The single highest-leverage move is an off-site campaign to flood neutral compare-stage sources (review platforms, finance media, affiliate roundups) with quantified BILL data, paired with a canonical plain-text pricing table to end the three-different-answers pricing problem.

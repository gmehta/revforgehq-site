4Brand Sentinel — what AI likely tells your buyers Risk findings

Competitors own the Brivo pricing answer

When users ask engines "how much does Brivo cost," the dollar figures ($13.50 first two doors, ~$250/mo Professional base, ~$580/mo Enterprise) are sourced from Kisi, ACRE Security, getsafeandsound and similar third-party/competitor pages — not brivo.com. Third-party ranges disagree ($12–$25/door, plus $5K–$150K implementation estimates), so the AI-served price of Brivo is set by rivals and varies by source.
→ Fix: publish an owned, crawlable pricing page with edition pricing, typical hardware/install ranges, and a TCO explainer; reference it from llms.txt.

Mobile app complaints surface directly in AI answers

The scan's own verbatim: Perplexity tells buyers Brivo app reviews "complain about an outdated interface, reliability problems, delayed door access, and occasional wrong lock-status displays," citing App Store reviews. Capterra reviews echo "phone app is not user-friendly."
→ Fix: prioritize app-store rating recovery (release notes, review responses, prompt happy users) — App Store listings are a cited source in validate answers.

"Predatory" billing narrative forming in review UGC

Capterra reviewers describe forced hardware upgrades followed by ~30% monthly fee increases as "predatory business practices," and search results flag "unexpected price increases and inconsistent customer support as recurring concerns." This language is exactly what engines quote in worth-it answers.
→ Fix: publish a price-change/renewal policy page and respond to the specific reviews; give engines an owned counter-source.

Eagle Eye merger creates entity confusion

Homepage banner says Brivo and Eagle Eye Networks "have merged" and "while we work to update our website, go to EEN.com" — two brands, two domains, partially updated site. Engines may split or mis-attribute the entity; scan answers still describe Brivo standalone.
→ Fix: ship consistent Organization schema and an updated About/press narrative linking Brivo ↔ Eagle Eye Networks across both domains.

6Strategic insight — the pitch

Brivo wins decisively once it's in the consideration set — 100% mention on compare and validate prompts, with engines pulling accurate compliance, integration, and hardware facts from owned pages and the llms.txt. It loses at discovery (37.5%) and on pricing, where competitors' "Brivo cost" pages are the cited source. The single highest-leverage move: publish an owned, crawlable pricing/TCO page and category-discovery content ("best cloud access control") so engines stop sourcing Brivo's price and category framing from Avigilon, Kisi, and ACRE.

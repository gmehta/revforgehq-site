What AI tells Fabletics customers

We asked ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI the questions your buyers actually ask — about leggings, memberships, and whether Fabletics can be trusted — and audited what they answer, who they cite, and where the answers are wrong.

The 30-second version

AI is quoting your membership price wrong

Perplexity tells buyers VIP membership is "$59.95 per month" — you charge $69.95. Customers arrive expecting $10 less, then feel tricked. This single error feeds the "scam" narrative more than any competitor does.

"Not a scam, but…" is your AI reputation

Asked "Is Fabletics legit?", engines answer it's "not a straight-up scam, but widely criticized for a confusing VIP subscription" — and cite class-action sites and complaint services in billing answers. Your product praise is real but arrives wrapped in warnings.

Three fixes change this

1) A canonical, schema-marked membership page with the real price — kills the $59.95 error.
2) Structured data + answer-first content so engines cite you, not help-desk articles and YouTube.
3) Monthly re-scans to prove the answers moved.

The 2026 multiplier: agentic checkout

AI engines are becoming AI shopping agents that compare, decide and transact on the buyer's behalf. A human might shrug off a $10 discrepancy — an agent instructed to "buy if it's $59.95" simply abandons, at machine scale, with no human to win back. Fixing the canonical price signal now is cheap; renegotiating with thousands of autonomous checkouts later is not.

Why this is happening — site & narrative audit

Your site gives AI almost nothing to cite

Verified Jun 10: zero structured data (JSON-LD), zero social/OG tags, no H1 heading, 88 of 194 images without alt text, no llms.txt — the homepage is a rotating promo wall. Engines fall back to YouTube, blogs and help-desk articles.

→ Schema rollout + canonical membership page + llms.txt

Product praise is real, but entangled

Fabletics IS in 2026 "best leggings" lists (PowerHold praised by NBC, CNN, Athletech). But nearly every favorable mention carries a subscription caveat that AI merges into one warning-laden recommendation.

→ Decouple: product/fabric content separate from a fixed membership answer

The billing narrative is authored by critics

Reddit, Trustpilot, BBB and 2015-era press (CA district-attorney coverage) own "is Fabletics a scam." You've since added checkout warnings and online cancellation — but no crawlable page tells that story, so engines cite the old one.

→ Transparency hub: plain-text answers to cancel/skip/refund questions

Entity defense — overwriting a stale narrative at the source

Fixing fabletics.com is necessary but not sufficient: the billing story AI tells is assembled from off-site sources — BBB complaint pages, topclassactions.com, consumeraffairs.com, Trustpilot, and 2015-era press. Those pages won't update themselves. Entity reclamation works the problem from both ends:

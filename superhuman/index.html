4Brand Sentinel — what AI likely tells your buyers Risk findings

Rebrand identity confusion is live in AI answers right now

OpenAI's answer to "What companies use Superhuman?" lists Grammarly as a customer of Superhuman (stale theirstack data) — when Grammarly IS Superhuman. Searches confirm subscribers report confusion at being billed by "Superhuman Platform" for a Grammarly purchase, and the homepage privacy link still points to grammarly.com. → Fix: publish a canonical, crawlable "Grammarly is now Superhuman" entity page + Organization JSON-LD with sameAs links binding Grammarly/Superhuman/Coda entities; update Wikipedia/Wikidata.

Two parallel pricing systems are producing contradictory AI answers

Scan verbatims show engines mixing Suite pricing (Free / Pro $12 / Business $33 annual) with Superhuman Mail pricing (Starter $25–30 / Business $40) in single answers; third parties quote $30/mo, $300/yr, "17% discount = $25/mo." A buyer asking "How much does Superhuman cost?" gets three different ladders. → Fix: one canonical pricing page with both products clearly separated in crawlable text, and align help.superhuman.com Pricing-Plans article exactly to it.

Post-acquisition sentiment: slow AI, mobile "an afterthought," continuity fears

Trustpilot reviews (Jul 2025 onward) report "extremely slow AI features causing delays opening emails"; Reddit threads call mobile an afterthought; Hacker News (3 citations in this scan) airs fears the product gets "abandoned post-acquisition." The "$30/month is too expensive for most people" frame is already Perplexity's default verdict. → Fix: publish a dated performance/roadmap commitment post and a "worth it for whom" ROI page that owns the value-justification answer.

March 2026 "Expert Review" backlash attaches reputational risk to the new brand

The AI feature simulating real (living and deceased) authors' writing advice without permission drew public criticism and complaints in Mar 2026 — directional, but it is exactly the kind of controversy engines surface on "Superhuman reviews/criticism" prompts. → Fix: ensure an official, crawlable response/policy page exists so AI answers cite Superhuman's position, not only the criticism.

6Strategic insight — the pitch

Superhuman dominates compare and validate prompts (100% presence) and its owned domains supply 54 of the top citations — but it enters this rebrand with no llms.txt, a YouTube citation footprint larger than its own site, and engines actively confusing Grammarly-the-acquirer with Grammarly-the-product. The single highest-leverage move is an entity-consolidation package: llms.txt + canonical rebrand/pricing pages + structured data that tells every engine, in one crawlable place, what "Superhuman" now means and what it costs — before stale data and competitor content harden into the default answer.
